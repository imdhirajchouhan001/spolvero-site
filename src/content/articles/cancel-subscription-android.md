Most of the frustration around cancelling a subscription on Android comes from one wrong assumption: that the app you installed and the subscription you pay for are the same thing. They are not. The app is on your phone. The subscription is a billing arrangement held by Google, or by the company, or occasionally by both.

That is why deleting the app does nothing, and why some subscriptions are missing from the list people are told to check.

## Cancelling a Google Play subscription

Four taps, and it is the same on every Android phone.

1. Open the **Play Store**.
2. Tap your **profile picture**, top right.
3. **Payments & subscriptions** → **Subscriptions**.
4. Pick the subscription, then **Cancel subscription**.

You can also do it in a browser at `play.google.com/store/account/subscriptions`, which is easier if you are cancelling several at once.

Google's guidance is to cancel at least **24 hours before** the renewal date. Leave it to the morning of and you can find the charge has already gone through.

### What cancelling actually does

**You keep access until the period you have paid for ends.** Cancelling on day three of a monthly subscription does not end it on day three. You keep it until day thirty and then it stops. There is no reason to wait until the last day, and waiting is how people miss it.

**It does not refund anything.** Cancelling stops the next charge. It does not undo the last one.

**It is reversible until the end date.** A cancelled subscription usually shows a Resubscribe option while the paid period is still running.

## The subscriptions Play does not show you

This is the part that catches people, and it is the reason a Play list can look complete while money keeps leaving.

A subscription only appears in Play if it was **bought through Play**. Plenty are not:

- **Bought on the company's website.** Streaming services, news sites and software companies often push you to sign up in a browser precisely to avoid Play's cut. Those are billed directly to your card and Google has no record of them.
- **Bought on another platform.** If you first subscribed on an iPhone, an iPad or a Mac, it is an Apple subscription. It will not show in Play even though you now use the app on Android.
- **Billed through a carrier.** Some services can be added to a phone bill. Those are cancelled through the network, not through Play or the company.
- **Bought through PayPal.** PayPal keeps its own list of recurring payments, under Settings → Payments → Automatic payments, and it is worth reading because things sit there that appear nowhere else.

If a charge is on your statement but not in your Play list, one of those four is why.

## Deleting the app does not cancel it

This is the single most expensive misunderstanding in the whole subject, and it is completely reasonable — uninstalling feels final.

It is not. The subscription is a billing agreement between you and Google or you and the company. Your phone is not part of it. You can uninstall the app, factory reset the phone, or throw it in a river, and the payment will still be taken next month.

The same goes for **deleting your account** with the service. Some companies cancel the subscription when you close an account; many do not, and a few make a point of telling you in the confirmation email that they are separate actions. Cancel the subscription first, then close the account.

## Cancelling one that was not bought through Play

Work through it in this order.

**Find the receipt.** Search your email for the service name. The receipt almost always names who took the money — Google, Apple, PayPal, or the company itself — and that tells you where to cancel.

**Look for account settings, not support.** Nearly every service has a cancel path inside account settings. It is usually two or three levels down under "Plan", "Membership" or "Billing". It is rarely on the first screen, and that is deliberate.

**Use the web version, not the app.** Some apps have no cancel option at all because they do not want one. The same account in a browser usually does.

**If you genuinely cannot find it,** email support and say plainly that you are cancelling, with the date. In the UK and EU that creates a record, which matters if a charge appears afterwards.

## Cancelling your card does not cancel the contract

A tempting shortcut that causes more problems than it solves.

Blocking the payment stops the money, but the agreement stands. The service can pursue the debt, some pass it to collections, and card networks have mechanisms for updating a merchant with your new card number automatically — which is how a "cancelled" card sometimes keeps paying.

Cancel properly, then block the card only if a charge still appears after you have.

## Refunds

Worth knowing what is realistic. Google runs a short self-service refund window through the Play Store, and after that the decision passes to the developer. Beyond both, you are relying on goodwill or on your card issuer.

The practical consequence is that **noticing early is what gets your money back.** A charge spotted the same day is often refundable; the same charge spotted in the next statement usually is not.
